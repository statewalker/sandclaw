# Notifications

Things happen while the person looks elsewhere: a task finishes or waits for an approval, a site
is built, indexing ends or some files can't be read, the Sandclaw machine goes offline and comes
back. Admins also hear about the team (an invite accepted or declined) and the services (one is
down). One kind is different: **a device added to my identity**. It is the only sign that
someone else may be using the person's keys, so it is always shown and cannot be silenced.

## Where a notice shows

Each kind has a setting: **Bell + toast**, **Bell only**, or **Off**.

- **The bell list** keeps the notice. It is the record: the last 30 days, at most 100 notices,
  kept in this browser only (another device keeps its own list).
- **A toast** says it now, with one action ("Open", "Review", "Team"). In the app it leaves after
  a few seconds, except a task waiting for you and a device added, which stay until answered or
  dismissed. The prototype keeps every toast until dismissed.
- **A system notification** replaces the toast when the tab is in the background and the browser
  allows it; a toast in a hidden tab would be missed. Without permission the toast waits for the
  person to come back. The settings show the ask and its states.

The defaults put routine news in the bell only (indexing finished, files unreadable, an invite
declined) and everything that changes what the person does next in a toast too.

**Repeats merge.** Notices of one kind within an hour of each other become one line, "3 tasks
finished", listing their titles; the same rule merges toasts. Security notices never merge: each
device added is its own line.

**Unread.** The badge counts unread lines (a merged line counts once) that arrived since the bell
was last opened. Opening the bell clears the badge; the lines stay marked unread until opened or
"Mark all read". Opening a line or a toast's action marks its notices read.

## A — Bell dropdown, toasts bottom-right

A bell next to the group's status opens a dropdown over the work, closed by Escape or a click
outside. Toasts stack bottom-right, each with a title, a detail and its action. Familiar from
every web app, and it keeps the workspace layout untouched; the list is a glance, not a place to
work from.

## B — Activity panel, minimal toasts

The bell opens an **Activity** panel docked beside the documents (in the app, a dockview panel
the person can move or keep open); at phone width it covers the work. Toasts are a single line
at the bottom centre, and only for what needs the person: a task failed or waiting, a device
added, the machine offline, a service down. The rest only bumps the badge.

Fewer interruptions, and a list that can stay open while working. It costs a panel slot, and a
finished task goes unnoticed until the person looks.

**Leaning: A**, with B's rule for which kinds toast by default. The dropdown needs no layout
decision, and the list is short-lived reading, not a workspace.

## Open questions

- Should the badge count unread lines or only what needs the person (waiting, failed, security)?
  Leaning: all unread lines, capped at "9+".
- Is "kept in this browser" enough, or should the list follow the person across devices through
  the machine? Leaning: this browser for now; a device added is also recorded on the identity.
- Does "machine back online" need its own notice, or should it replace the "offline" line in
  place? Leaning: replace it, one line "Offline 08:00–10:00 · 2 tasks resumed".
- Should "Off" still keep the notice in the list (silent but recorded)? Leaning: no, Off means
  not recorded; the tasks strip and logs keep the history.
- Ask for browser notifications on first need (first toast in a hidden tab) or only from
  settings? Leaning: from settings, plus a one-time hint on the first task waiting for you.
- Should a task waiting for you also show in the tab title ("(1) Sandclaw")? Leaning: yes.
