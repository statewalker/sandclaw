# Settings — one place for me and, for admins, the group

Every setting lives in one place, split in two groups:

- **You** — Me (name, devices, the browser lock, leaving), Appearance, Notifications,
  Connectors, Agent tools, This browser (the data kept here), What the assistant reads.
- **Your group — admin** — Team, Invitations, Services, AI models.

A member does not see the admin group at all: not greyed out, not "ask your admin", just absent.
The sections are the screens the other prototypes built, embedded unchanged; Appearance is the
only new one.

The section is a **deep link**: Settings opens at the section it is given (`section: "team"`),
so a notification "Hugo joined" opens Settings at Team, and "This browser is nearly full" at
This browser. A link the viewer may not open (a member sent to Team) or that names no section
opens the first section, Me, instead of an error.

## Indexing is the member's, not the admin's

"What the assistant reads" sits under **You**. The assistant reads the folder in *this browser*:
each person's browser reads its own copy, and turning a folder off only changes what *my*
assistant answers from. Nothing on that screen changes anything for anyone else, so there is
nothing for an admin to own. What is group-wide about reading — which model turns files into
meaning (the embeddings model) — is already in AI models, which stays admin.

## A — A full page

Settings replaces the workspace; "← Workspace" goes back. Room for the wide screens (Team, AI
models, Services) without a scroll inside a scroll, and the address is the deep link: a
notification, a bookmark, a link pasted in chat all land on the right section. The cost: the
document I was editing is out of sight while I change a setting about it.

<Story of={Stories.Page} inline={false} height="820px" />

## B — A dialog over the workspace

The gear opens Settings in a large dialog; the workspace stays as it was underneath and comes
back on Escape. Good for a quick change (dark theme, a connector's sign-in) and for the deep link
from a notification, which should not throw away the layout. The cost: the wide admin screens
are cramped, they scroll inside the dialog, and their own dialogs (remove a person, switch a
model) open on top of a dialog.

<Story of={Stories.InDialog} inline={false} height="820px" />

**Leaning:** A. Most settings are visited rarely and the admin screens are pages in their own
right. The third option, Settings as a tab in the workspace's center zone, was not built: it
puts Settings among the documents, where it is closed by accident, opened twice, and saved into
the dock layout; and on a phone the center zone is a single view anyway, which is A.

## Search

The search matches section names and a few words people use for what is inside: "lock" finds Me
(the browser lock), "token" or "cost" finds AI models, "printer" finds Services, "dark" finds
Appearance. A match by keyword says which word matched, so "Me" for "lock" is not a mystery. It
matches the start of words ("lock" does not find Agent tools' "block"), ignores case and accents,
and only searches sections the viewer can see. Enter opens the first result.

<Story of={Stories.SearchLock} inline={false} height="820px" />

"trash" finds nothing: the trash is in the folder (the file manager), not a setting.

## Open questions

- A page or a dialog? Leaning: page, with the dialog reserved for nothing.
- Should "trash" (and other words for things outside Settings) point to where they are, instead
  of "No settings match"? Leaning: yes, a short "Not a setting — the trash is in Files" line.
- Does Appearance follow me across devices? Leaning: no, per browser.
- Is "Me" the right name, or "My identity and devices"? Leaning: "Me" in the list, the full title
  on the page.
- Should a member see a read-only Team (who is in the group)? Leaning: yes, but outside Settings,
  in the group status.
- Does searching also search *inside* sections (a connector's name, a person)? Leaning: not yet.
