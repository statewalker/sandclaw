# Site table of contents

A thematic site ("Dupont renovation", "How we work") is a static site the assistant writes from
the folder, one page per section. The table of contents decides the pages: their titles, their
order, which pages sit under which, and what each page is written **from**: topics that Ask's
indexing found (*Kitchen works*, 14 documents) and specific files (*Dupont — offer.docx*).

Common to both variants:

- **A section is a page.** Two levels only, pages and the pages under them, because that is
  what a site menu can show without a second menu. A move that would make a third level is
  refused, and the line under the tree says why.
- **A section with no sources can't be written.** Its row has a warning sign and its sources
  say so in words: "The assistant has nothing to write this page from. Add a topic or a file."
  It can still be saved (a placeholder page for later); *Save and rewrite* skips it and says so.
- **Rename in place**: F2, a double click, or *Rename* in the "…" menu; an empty title is
  refused under the field. **Add** goes after the selected section, or under it from the menu.
- **Remove** asks first only when something would be lost: a section with pages under it
  ("Remove “The works” and 2 pages under it?") or with sources. An empty section goes at once,
  with *Undo*. The confirmation says the files and topics stay: only the page leaves the site.
- **Reorder** by dragging: the upper or lower third of a row puts it before or after (a line
  shows where), the middle puts it under (the row is outlined), and the line under the tree
  says it in words ("Move “Photos” under “Schedule”"). Targets that would break the two levels,
  or put a section inside itself, don't light up. By keyboard: Alt+↑ ↓ swap with the
  neighbour, Alt+→ goes under the section above, Alt+← comes back out, right after its parent.
- **Sources** are attached from a searchable picker: Ask's topics with their document counts,
  or the folder's files with the folder they are in. Each click attaches or detaches; the page
  shows them as chips with a × to remove.
- **What saving changes.** A section whose title or sources changed, or a new one, is marked
  *Will be rewritten*; a removed one is listed as "taken off the site". A move changes the menu
  only, so it rewrites nothing. *Save* keeps the new contents and leaves those pages marked
  until they are rewritten; *Save and rewrite 3 pages* starts the writing. Rewriting is the
  slow, costly part, so it is never a side effect of saving.

## A — Tree and details

The tree on the left, the selected section's title and sources on the right; on a narrow screen
the details go under the tree. The details have room to explain (the warning, "This page will
be rewritten", the pages under it), and the title is an ordinary text field, which is what a
non-technical user tries first. The cost: one section's sources at a time, and two places to
look.

## B — Outline, sources in place

One outline; a row opens in place to show its sources as chips. Several sections can be open
at once, so comparing "Kitchen" with "The works" is one look. It is denser and closer to an
outliner, which suits people who think in lists; a long site becomes a long scroll, and the
title is edited only in place.

<Story of={Stories.Outline} inline={false} height="620px" />

**Leaning A** for members: the details pane is where the "why can't this page be written?"
question gets answered, and its layout survives a phone by stacking.

## Starting empty: Suggest from topics

An empty contents offers to start from Ask's topics: one section per theme Ask grouped them in,
written from those topics. On an empty contents there is nothing to lose, so the suggestion is
applied at once, with *Undo*.

<Story of={Stories.Empty} inline={false} height="420px" />

## A suggestion over existing contents

On contents the user already edited, *Suggest from topics* never replaces anything. It shows
only what it would **add**, each accepted on its own or all together: topics for a section
whose title matches (ignoring case and accents), or a new section. A topic the user already
placed somewhere is not offered again, and a section the user renamed or made up stays as it
is (renamed "Schedule" to "Planning" and the suggestion offers a new "Schedule"; that is the
one rough edge, see the questions).

## Phone

Touch screens have no drag and drop in the browser. The "…" is always visible, rows are taller,
and its menu has *Move up*, *Move down* and *Move to…* (the top level, or under one of the
top-level sections). The details stack under the tree.

## Open questions

- A or B? Leaning A, for the room to explain a page that can't be written.
- Should a renamed page be rewritten, or only get its new title? Leaning: only the title and
  the menu change; rewrite only when sources change, and say so.
- When a suggested theme matches no title because the user renamed it, offer a new section or
  try to match by the topics it shares? Leaning: match by shared topics first, then by title.
- Can the same topic feed two pages ("Kitchen works" in "The works" and in "Kitchen")? Leaning
  yes, but show "also used in …" on the chip, since it usually means a page repeats another.
- A parent with pages under it: is it a page of its own, or only a heading in the menu?
  Leaning: a page (an overview of its children), so it needs sources like any other.
- Should the warning block *Save and rewrite*, or let the user save placeholder pages?
  Leaning: allow, skip them, and say how many were skipped.
- Alt+← is "Back" in some browsers when focus is outside the tree; is Alt+arrows the right
  reorder shortcut, or Ctrl+Shift+arrows as in Google Docs outlines? Leaning keep Alt (Workflowy,
  Notion), with the menu as the fallback.
